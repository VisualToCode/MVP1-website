Podcast

Every industry will be disrupted. The only question is whether you'll be the one doing it.

Jay Pandya sets out the four forces that have to converge before an industry turns over — and why watching any one of them in isolation is how incumbents get caught.

Jay Pandya5 May 201844 min

Kodak had the digital camera. Blockbuster had the customer base, the stores and the brand. Neither was undone by a single technology arriving. They were undone by four things arriving together and being read as four unrelated events.

The four forces

In this keynote, Jay argues that disruption is a convergence, not an invention. Four forces have to line up:

  1. Platform business models — value moving from a linear chain to an orchestrated network.
  2. Human experience design — expectations reset by whichever product treats people best, in any category.
  3. Emerging technology — capability that quietly becomes cheap enough to assume.
  4. Social trend shifts — what people are willing to do, share, trust and pay for.

Uber did not win on the technology. GPS, smartphones and payments were all available to the taxi industry. It won because a platform model, a redesigned experience, cheap ubiquitous technology and a change in what people would accept from a stranger's car all arrived at the same moment. Airbnb is the same story with a different asset class.

Disruption has never come from one technology alone. It never will.

Why incumbents miss it

Because each force on its own looks manageable. A new app is a competitor. A shift in customer expectation is a marketing problem. A cheaper technology is a procurement decision. It is only the convergence that is fatal, and convergence is exactly what a functional organisation is structured not to see — each force lands in a different department.

Leading it instead of surviving it

The practical half of the talk is a framework for founders and executives to work out where the four forces are converging on their own industry, and what to do about it early — while the question is still which business model to build, rather than how to defend the one you have.

Key takeaways

  • Disruption is a convergence of four forces, never a single technology.
  • Each force lands in a different department, which is why organisations miss the pattern.
  • Kodak and Blockbuster had the technology; they did not have the business model.
  • The useful question is where the four forces are converging on your industry now.
