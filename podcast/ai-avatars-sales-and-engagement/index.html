AI avatars: transforming sales and customer engagement

A short conversation on what happens to a sales function when the interaction stays human-shaped but the capacity stops being human-limited.

Jay Pandya25 July 202419 min

What if your top salesperson never slept, never missed a beat, and could scale instantly?

Every sales organisation has a version of the same ceiling. The best person on the team converts at a rate nobody else matches, and there is exactly one of them, working one time zone, for a bounded number of hours a week.

What actually scales

Jay's argument is that the scalable part is not the person, it is the pattern, the questions they ask, the order they ask them in, the objections they anticipate, the moment they stop selling and start listening. Those are learnable and reproducible. Rapport, judgement in an unusual situation, and knowing when a deal is not right are not.

Blending nuance with precision

The interesting engineering problem is the blend: enough human nuance that the interaction is worth having, enough machine precision that it is consistent and logged. Too far towards the machine and prospects disengage. Too far towards imitation and you end up in the uncanny valley, which is worse than an obvious bot.

Where it fits

At nineteen minutes this is the short one, a focused look at first-line engagement, qualification and follow-up, and where the handover to a person belongs.

Key takeaways

  • The scalable asset is the pattern the best seller follows, not the seller.
  • Rapport and judgement do not automate; sequence and consistency do.
  • Overshooting on imitation is worse than an obvious bot.
  • Avatars belong at first-line engagement, with a clear human handover.
